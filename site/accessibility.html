Accessibility

A C# IDE your screen reader can read

From 0.4.0, a screen reader reads Passenger's code editor and terminal as it reads a text field, hears what Passenger tells you, and finds its way round the window from the keyboard. This page says what each system gets today, and what isn't there yet.

Your screen reader, system by system

Passenger draws its own editor and terminal, so it gives each system's screen reader their text itself, through the system's own accessibility interfaces.

Windows: Narrator, NVDA and JAWS

The code editor and the terminal are read through UI Automation's text pattern, as a system text field is: by character, word, line and the whole text, following the caret and every selection, reviewing any line away from the caret, and finding text.

What Passenger tells you, the code's popups and every tree are heard too.

macOS: VoiceOver

The code editor and the terminal are read as a system text area: by character, word and line, following and announcing the selection, reviewing any line, and hearing what each key typed or deleted and where the caret went.

What Passenger tells you, the code's popups and every tree are heard too.

Linux: Orca, in part

Orca reads the line the caret is on in the code editor and the terminal, again as the caret moves to another line or the line changes, and every tree with each row's level and place.

Reading by character and word and following the selection aren't there yet on Linux, and what Passenger tells you reaches Orca only as changed text, which it may not read out.

Heard as you work

What changes on the screen as you work is told to your screen reader, while the keys stay where you are.

What Passenger tells you
The status bar's messages, a refusal or a failure at once, each stage of a long operation and what it came to, and a notification or an agent's question as it comes, each heard once, your keys staying where they were.
Completion
The item chosen as the list opens, narrows and moves (“Completion, Equals, method, item 1 of 4”), and that it closed. Quick Documentation with the list open reads the chosen item's documentation.
Parameter Info
The signature as it shows, then the parameter the caret reaches as you type a comma or move through the call.
Cards and fixes
A card a key opens, Quick Documentation or Quick Evaluate Expression, is read as it shows, and the list of fixes and refactorings speaks as you move through it.
Trees
The Explorer and the Solution view, Unit Tests, Problems, Git's branches, Debug's variables and the profiler's calls are trees to a screen reader: each row's level, its place among the rows beside it, and whether it is open.
Read as Text
Ctrl+Alt+R (Ctrl+Cmd+R on the Mac) opens what the part with the keys says as plain text to read line by line: the problems on the caret's line and its card, the terminal's last command and its output, a diff's changes, a test's result, the problem chosen in Problems or the last message. Escape gives the keys back.
Sound cues
A short tone as the caret reaches a line with an error, a warning, a breakpoint, a fold or a change, and as a build or a test run passes or fails, the debugger stops your program, a command fails in the terminal or a save is refused. They play while a screen reader runs, or always, and Settings › Accessibility turns each on or off.

Round the window from the keyboard

Every part of the work above is reached from the keyboard, and so is every part of the window.

Regions
Next Region (Ctrl+Alt+` on Windows and Linux, Ctrl+Cmd+` on the Mac) and Previous Region, the same with Shift, go round the toolbar, the tool windows, the editors and the status bar in the order they stand, each named as you enter it: “Explorer, tool window”, “Notes.txt, editor”.
Every action by name
Find Action runs any of the IDE's actions by its name, and Search Everywhere, Go to File and Go to Symbol find the rest.
Lists
Enter does what a double click does in every list, and Space ticks a row in the Commit window.
Shortcuts at your pace
Settings' Wait for a shortcut's second key gives a two-key shortcut up to a minute between its keys. The keys follow Visual Studio's, VS Code's or IntelliJ IDEA's keymap, and Vim mode is a setting away.

Easier to see

For low vision, and for eyes that tire: contrast, size and motion.

Readable contrast
Passenger's own theme, dark and light, meets WCAG 2.2 AA wherever it draws: 4.5:1 for words, 3:1 for icons, the caret, focus rings and fields' edges, held by a test.
High Contrast
A High Contrast theme, dark and light, puts every word at WCAG AAA's 7:1 and every mark and edge at 4.5:1, and takes over while Windows' contrast themes, macOS's Increase contrast or GNOME's High Contrast is on.
Not by colour alone
Errors, warnings and suggestions are underlined in shapes of their own, added, changed and conflicted lines are marked in different shapes, a diff signs its lines and a changed file shows its letter. Settings › Appearance › Accessibility › Colours of states lays a colour-blind safe palette over any theme.
Where you are
The chosen row, the open tab and the open tool window carry an accent mark at 3:1 beside their tinted ground, never the tint alone.
Larger
Settings › Appearance's display scale draws the whole interface larger on top of your system's scaling, and the code's size and font are yours to choose.
Steady
Passenger follows your system's motion and caret settings on macOS, Windows and GNOME: with less motion asked, flings, the working ring and progress bars hold still, and carets stay steady or blink at your rate. Caret width sets how broad the caret is, the terminal's cursor too.
